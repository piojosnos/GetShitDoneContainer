## Phase Goal

ROADMAP.md Phase 4 goal (verbatim): "The user gets into any registered sandbox with one command, and can see and clean up their sandboxes without risking code, login, or history."

Not in user-story form; quoted verbatim as in earlier phases.

This plan lets the user clean up: `sbx-rm NAME` removes a sandbox's container, and with `--unregister` its registry entry, while every file of the sandbox stays on the Mac. The one guard rule that kept every delete out of `bin/` is rewritten in the same commit as `bin/sbx-rm`: the rule as it stands rejects the new script (its name holds the word rm, and it runs a compose down), and a narrower rule for that one file keeps the protection.

<assumption_delta_decision>
- Signal: kind `optional`, "The user removes a sandbox's container, and optionally its registration."
- Primary noun: the registry entry `~/.config/sbx/sandboxes/NAME` stays the identity of a sandbox; the container `sbx-NAME` is a disposable instance of it, and the folder `SBX_DIR` holds its data.
- Decision: no-change.
- Rationale: `--unregister` is one extra flag that deletes the identity after its instance is gone; no field of the model becomes optional, and `sbx-add` of the same folder recreates the same identity, so no anchor moves.
</assumption_delta_decision>

## Conventions carried into every edit

- Comment shape: a banner is a rule line of `# ` plus 78 dashes (80 columns), a title line, a rule line, then at most two "why" lines and a closing rule; with no why, the title sits between two rules. No comment line over 80 columns: shorten a title in this plan if it does not fit, keeping its meaning.
- Shell: `set -eu` in the bin scripts, `set -u` in tests; libraries set no options and run nothing; functions plus a `# Main / Entry Point` banner over a short list of calls; braces always; a blank line before and after every `if`/`for`/`while`/`case` block (an `else` stays cuddled); full camelCase variable names; list variables end in `List`.
- Stock bash 3.2 and BSD tools: no arrays in the argument parser (bash 3.2 calls an empty array unbound under `set -u`), no `mapfile`, `declare -A`, `${var,,}`, `&>>`, `|&`, `[[ -v ]]`, `sed -i`, `readlink -f`, `stat -c`; test `$#` before reading `$2`.
- Quote every expansion; host paths may hold spaces. Every docker call reads `</dev/null`.
- Every `bin/` file other than `bin/sbx-rm` keeps the broad rule: its text never contains the words rm, rmdir, prune, kill, down or eval, nor the option --volumes, nor the text sbx-rm.
- `bin/sbx-rm` keeps the narrow rule in all its text, comments included: no rmdir, prune, kill or eval; no option --volumes, --rmi or --remove-orphans; no word down followed by an option, and no -v option anywhere; and the word rm used as a word of its own (not inside sbx-rm) appears on exactly one line, the delete line given in Task 1. Its comments therefore say "delete" or "removal", never rm on its own.
- ASCII only. No em or en dash, no ` -- ` or spaced ` - ` used as a dash; punctuation after a closing quote. The `--` before the delete operand is code, not a dash.
- No planning IDs in committed source, tests, comments or commit messages. Host check IDs such as H-33 are product IDs and are fine.
